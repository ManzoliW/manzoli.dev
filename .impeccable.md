# manzoli.dev

Personal site for William Manzoli — Senior Frontend TypeScript Engineer based in São Paulo, BR. Single-page Nuxt 3 app deployed to Vercel Edge.

## Design Context

### Users
Two audiences, weighted equally:

1. **Hiring managers and recruiters** evaluating William for senior frontend roles. They arrive from LinkedIn / a referral / a CV link and need to confirm seniority and shipping track record within the first viewport, then find a way to reach out.
2. **Fellow engineers** who land here from GitHub, a Twitter/X link, or a conference bio. They are judging taste and craft as much as content; "this person ships and has taste" is the takeaway to engineer for.

The site succeeds when a recruiter books a call *and* an engineer remembers it a week later.

### Brand Personality
**Playful · tactile · unexpected.**

Voice is low-volume and editorial — short uppercase mono eyebrows (`OPEN SOURCE`, `TRUSTED BY`, `WORDS`), serif sentences that read like a magazine standfirst ("Built in public.", "Quietly shipping for teams that ship a lot."). The playfulness lives in the *interactions*, not the copy: peeling stickers, a WebGL prismatic burst behind every section, roman-numeral pagination, variable-proximity headlines, wheel/keyboard/touch nav. The interface should feel like a physical artifact — paper, stickers, ink — not a SaaS dashboard.

### Aesthetic Direction
**Warm editorial canvas with tactile surprise.**

- **Type**: Newsreader (serif display) for headlines, Inter (sans) for body, JetBrains Mono for eyebrows / labels / metadata. Mono is used *editorially* (small caps tracking, uppercase) — never as a "developer" affectation on body copy.
- **Palette**: Warm paper tones are the canvas — `page #e8e2d3`, `paper #f5f1e6`, `ink #1a1612`. The third section (Engineer / Work) flips to a deep teal `#003c33` with paper text; this is the *only* dark surface, not a global dark mode.
- **Composition**: A single full-viewport rounded card with two halves (WebGL left, content right). Three swappable sections inside that card. Asymmetric, left-aligned, never centered prose.
- **Motion**: Every animated element is wrapped in `<ClientOnly>` and gated by `useReducedMotion`. Transitions use ease, not bounce/elastic. Wheel-nav is debounced 700ms.
- **Anti-references** (explicit do-not-imitates):
  - Linear / Vercel / Stripe template clones — dark mode + neon gradient + glass card SaaS aesthetic.
  - Generic dev portfolio template — timeline + skill bars + project-card grid.

### Design Principles
1. **Tactility over chrome.** Reach for physical metaphors — paper, stickers, peeling, ink, roman numerals — before reaching for glass, glow, or gradient. Stickers have shadow + lighting intensity props for a reason.
2. **Editorial bones, playful detail.** Structure stays magazine-quiet (mono eyebrow → serif headline → sans body). Surprise lives in micro-interactions: a sticker that peels, a headline whose tracking responds to the cursor, a backdrop that breathes color.
3. **Warm canvas, never default-dark.** Paper tones are the home state. The teal section is a deliberate flip, not a toggle. Never introduce a global dark mode or neon accents.
4. **Motion must earn its frame.** Wrap in `<ClientOnly>`, gate with `useReducedMotion`, decelerate with ease (not bounce). If a motion can't be turned off cleanly, it doesn't ship.
5. **The card is a stage, not a container.** One vessel per viewport. Do not nest cards. Do not introduce a card grid of equal tiles. Footers (`STACK`, `TRUSTED BY`) pin to the bottom of the card; the middle scrolls.

### Accessibility Bar
WCAG 2.2 **AA**. Concretely:
- Body text contrast ≥ 4.5:1 in both the paper and teal sections.
- All interactive elements reachable by keyboard with a visible focus ring; navigation works without mouse or touch.
- `prefers-reduced-motion` short-circuits PrismaticBurst, SplitText, VariableProximity, StickerPeel, and LogoLoop to static fallbacks.
- Decorative WebGL backdrops are `aria-hidden` and `pointer-events: none` so they never block AT or interaction.
- Images and stickers have alt / aria-label text; the avatar PNG is decorative-paired with a heading, not the sole bearer of identity.
